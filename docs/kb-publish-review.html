What changed, and what's left

Applied to every article

  • Dropped the dead Acknowledge SOP links to corporate SharePoint and the “add a comment below to acknowledge” lines — Mark as read is the acknowledgment now.
  • Rewrote every summary. They were all the first 200 characters of the body, which read badly on the category cards and in the search results.
  • Rebuilt the numbered procedures. The SharePoint import stripped the step headings out of several SOPs, leaving orphaned bullets — Customer Check-In and Device Identification were the worst.
  • Added a Related block with working cross-links, and turned the !> callout on for the lines that are actually warnings.
  • Checked every article against the KB's renderer: no blockquotes, no bold spanning an asterisk, no mailto: links — all three render as raw text.

Facts I changed, not just formatting

  • Price Quoting & SKUs was a full copy of the price tables, already drifting from the Price Guide (the Pixel row disagreed). It's now the quoting rules — the $XX.99 convention, the parts-plus-labor formulas — pointing at the Price Guide for numbers.
  • Daily Inventory Count said to get the day's list from a Teams channel. It now points at the Consumption Report, including Send to RepairQ and the range mode for missed days.
  • Micro Soldering Agreement now describes sending the agreement through the Contracts tool rather than the old Square flow.
  • Express Repairs and Device & Port Cleanings pointed commission tracking at a Teams channel and Monday.com. Both now point at My Commission.

Two things worth your eye

A conflict between two policies. Assurant Claim Cancellation says nobody cancels a claim without your approval, “for any reason.” Claim Inventory Management tells staff to cancel a claim outright at the seven-day no-show mark. I read those as different situations and cross-linked them rather than picking a winner — but if a no-show cancellation also needs your sign-off, say so and I'll tighten the wording.

Nothing was announced. Publishing through the KB page posts to the Communications feed once per article, so doing it that way would have put 43 posts in everyone's feed this afternoon. These went in quietly. Once you've reviewed, I can post a single “the knowledge base is filled in” note — or set specific articles to require acknowledgment, which is the version that actually fires alerts.
